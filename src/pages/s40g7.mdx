<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-intro">
  <h2>Introduction</h2>
  <p>
    When a GPU renders a 3D scene, it needs to apply textures to objects. But what happens when a texture is viewed at different distances or angles? The GPU uses <strong>texture filtering</strong> to determine pixel colors — and two of the most fundamental methods are <strong>Nearest Neighbor</strong> and <strong>Bilinear Filtering</strong>.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-nearest">
  <h2>Nearest Neighbor Interpolation</h2>
  <p class="exhibit-lead">
    <strong>How it works:</strong> The GPU simply picks the closest texel to the sample point. No averaging, no blending.
  </p>
  <p class="exhibit-lead">
    <strong>Formula:</strong> <code>P(x,y) = P(round(x), round(y))</code>
  </p>

  <div class="exhibit-pros-cons">
    <div class="exhibit-pros-cons__col">
      <h3>Advantages</h3>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Fastest</strong> filtering method</li>
        <li>Simple to implement</li>
        <li>Preserves sharp edges</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="exhibit-pros-cons__col">
      <h3>Disadvantages</h3>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Blocky</strong>, pixelated appearance when zoomed in</li>
        <li>Noticeable aliasing (jagged edges)</li>
        <li>Poor quality for realistic images</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>

  <h3>Where it's used</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Pixel art games (like Minecraft)</li>
    <li>Retro-style games</li>
    <li>Older hardware</li>
  </ul>

<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-bilinear">
  <h2>Bilinear Filtering</h2>
  <div class="exhibit-bilinear-grid">
    <div class="exhibit-bilinear-text">
      <p>
        <strong>How it works:</strong> The GPU averages the 4 nearest texels (a 2×2 grid) and blends them together.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Formula:</strong> <code>P(x,y) = (TL + TR + BL + BR) / 4</code>
      </p>

      <div class="exhibit-pros-cons">
        <div class="exhibit-pros-cons__col">
          <h3>Advantages</h3>
          <ul>
            <li><strong>Smooth</strong> appearance</li>
            <li>No obvious pixelation</li>
            <li>Better visual quality</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
        <div class="exhibit-pros-cons__col">
          <h3>Disadvantages</h3>
          <ul>
            <li><strong>Slower</strong> than Nearest Neighbor</li>
            <li>Can appear slightly blurry</li>
            <li>Uses more GPU resources</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3>Where it's used</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Most modern games</li>
        <li>3D applications</li>
        <li>Textures viewed at normal distances</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

<section class="exhibit-section exhibit-supplementary">
  <h2>Comparison Table</h2>

| Feature | Nearest Neighbor | Bilinear |
|---------|------------------|----------|
| **Quality** | Low (blocky) | Medium (smooth) |
| **Performance** | Fastest | Good |
| **GPU Usage** | Very Low | Low |
| **Memory Usage** | None | None |
| **Formula** | Closest texel | (TL + TR + BL + BR) / 4 |

  <h2>When to Use Each Method</h2>

| Scenario | Recommended Method | Why |
|----------|-------------------|-----|
| **Pixel art games** | Nearest Neighbor | Preserves blocky aesthetic |
| **Retro style games** | Nearest Neighbor | Authentic retro look |
| **Modern 3D games** | Bilinear | Good balance of quality/speed |
| **VR/AR applications** | Anisotropic | Best quality for angles |
| **Mobile games** | Bilinear | Performance-focused |

  <h2>How This Works in the GPU Pipeline</h2>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Texture Loading</strong> — GPU loads texture into memory</li>
    <li><strong>UV Mapping</strong> — Texture coordinates are calculated</li>
    <li><strong>Sample Point</strong> — GPU finds where to sample on texture</li>
    <li><strong>Filtering Applied</strong> — Nearest or Bilinear (or other)</li>
    <li><strong>Pixel Output</strong> — Final color is displayed on screen</li>
  </ol>

  <h2>Fun Facts</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Nearest Neighbor</strong> was the default in early 3D games (like PlayStation 1)</li>
    <li><strong>Bilinear</strong> became popular with the PlayStation 2 era</li>
    <li>Modern GPUs can process <strong>100+ billion texels per second</strong></li>
    <li><strong>Anisotropic</strong> filtering (the next step up) was introduced in 1999 with NVIDIA GeForce 256</li>
  </ul>
